# ADR-0055: Outbound HTTP retry and timeout budget

**Status:** Active. Applies to every service making outbound HTTP calls, including
webhook delivery, third-party APIs and service-to-service traffic.

## Context

Retry policy was previously chosen per service, per client library, per engineer. Two
incidents were caused by services retrying each other into a synchronised load spike,
and one by a service that retried a non-idempotent call.

## Decision

Every outbound HTTP call is governed by a single budget:

| Parameter | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Total attempts | 3 | the original plus two retries, never more |
| Total time budget | 10 s | wall clock across all attempts, enforced by the caller |
| Backoff | exponential, base 200 ms | `200ms, 400ms` before attempts 2 and 3 |
| Jitter | full jitter | uniform in `[0, backoff]` — mandatory, not optional |
| Retry on | connect errors, `429`, `502`, `503`, `504` | |
| Never retry on | `4xx` other than `429`, pool acquisition timeouts, non-idempotent calls without an idempotency key | |

For asynchronous delivery — anything queued rather than awaited, webhook fan-out
included — the budget is different because the caller is not blocked:

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Total attempts | 8 |
| Schedule | 10 s, 30 s, 2 min, 10 min, 30 min, 2 h, 6 h, 24 h |
| Terminal action | move to the dead-letter queue, emit `delivery.failed` |
| Per-destination concurrency cap | 4 in flight |

After the eighth failed attempt the payload is not discarded. It goes to the
dead-letter queue and stays there for the retention window in the data retention policy.

## Consequences

The synchronous budget is deliberately tight. A caller that needs more than 10 seconds
should not be making a synchronous call.

The asynchronous schedule spans a full day, which means a destination that is down for an
afternoon recovers without operator involvement. It also means a duplicate is possible:
consumers must be idempotent. ADR-0048 covers idempotency keys.

Full jitter is the single most important line in this document. Without it, every caller
retries at the same moment and the retry storm is worse than the original failure.
