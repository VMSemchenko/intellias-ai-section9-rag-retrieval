# ADR-0044: Double-entry ledger schema

**Status:** Active.

## Context

The first ledger stored a single row per movement with a signed amount and a mutable
`balance` column on the account. Two concurrent writers could interleave and leave the
balance wrong, and there was no way to reconstruct how a balance had been reached.

## Decision

Strict double-entry. Two tables, both append-only.

    transaction(id, tenant_id, occurred_at, kind, external_ref, idempotency_key)
    entry(id, transaction_id, account_id, direction, amount_minor, currency)

Rules enforced in the database, not in application code:

1. Every transaction has at least two entries.
2. Per transaction and per currency, the sum of `debit` equals the sum of `credit`. A
   deferred constraint trigger rejects the transaction otherwise.
3. `entry` has no `UPDATE` or `DELETE` grant for the application role. A correction is a
   new, opposing transaction — never an edit.
4. `amount_minor` is a positive integer. Direction carries the sign. There are no negative
   amounts and no floating point anywhere in the schema.

Balances are derived, never stored:

    SELECT SUM(CASE WHEN direction = 'debit' THEN amount_minor ELSE -amount_minor END)
    FROM entry WHERE account_id = $1;

Hot accounts read from a materialised snapshot plus the entries since the snapshot, which
keeps the query bounded without making the balance authoritative anywhere but the entries.

## Consequences

The ledger is auditable by construction: any balance can be reproduced from the entries,
and no history can be rewritten.

Corrections are visible. A mistake and its reversal both appear on the statement. Finance
prefers this; several engineers have asked to "just fix the row" and the answer is no.

`external_ref` and `idempotency_key` let the ledger deduplicate a replayed request from
checkout, which is necessary because upstream delivery is at-least-once.
