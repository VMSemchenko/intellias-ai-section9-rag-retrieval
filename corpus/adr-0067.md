# ADR-0067: Reconciliation job scheduling

**Status:** Active.

## Context

The ledger must agree with the payment provider. Drift comes from timing (a capture we
recorded before the provider settled it), from provider-side corrections, and occasionally
from our own bugs. Finding drift a month later is expensive; finding it the next morning is
routine.

## Decision

Three jobs, all idempotent, all safe to re-run.

| Job | Schedule | Window | Action on mismatch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Provider settlement match | hourly at :20 | last 26 h | flag, do not correct |
| Internal invariant check | every 15 min | last 2 h | page on any imbalance |
| Daily close | 03:15 UTC | previous UTC day | flag, freeze the day, page |

The internal invariant check verifies that every transaction still balances and that no
entry row has been mutated. A failure here means either a bug or a direct database write,
and both are incidents.

Jobs never post correcting entries automatically. They flag. A human decides what the
correction is and it lands as a new transaction, per ADR-0044. Automated correction of a
financial record is not a trade-off we are willing to make.

The 26-hour window on the hourly job deliberately overlaps: each mismatch is seen roughly
26 times before it ages out, so one failed job run cannot hide a discrepancy.

## Consequences

Drift is detected within an hour in the normal case and within 15 minutes for internal
inconsistency. The June 2026 drift incident took six days to notice, which is what this
schedule exists to prevent.

Overlapping windows mean the same flag is raised repeatedly until resolved. The alert is
deduplicated by discrepancy fingerprint rather than by run, or the noise would be
unmanageable.

Manual reconciliation has its own runbook and requires two people for anything touching a
closed day.
