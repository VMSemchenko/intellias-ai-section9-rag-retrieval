# ADR-0022: Catalog search index refresh strategy

**Status:** Active.

## Context

Product data changes constantly — price, stock, description, availability per region. The
search index must reflect those changes without a full rebuild, which takes 40 minutes and
saturates the database read replica.

## Decision

Two paths into the index.

**Incremental, the normal path.** Every product mutation emits a change event. A consumer
re-indexes the affected product within a target of 30 seconds. This is a target, not a
guarantee: there is no transactional link between the product write and the index write.

**Full reindex, the exceptional path.** Run when the mapping changes, when the incremental
consumer has been down long enough to lose events, or when drift is detected. It builds
into a new index alias and swaps atomically; search stays available throughout. The
procedure is in the catalog reindex runbook.

Drift detection runs hourly: it compares a sampled count and checksum between the source
of truth and the index, and alerts above 0.1% divergence.

## Consequences

Search results can be up to 30 seconds stale under normal operation, and arbitrarily stale
if the incremental consumer is silently broken. The March 2026 postmortem is what happens
when the drift detector's alert is routed to a channel nobody reads.

Because indexing is asynchronous, a customer who edits a product and immediately searches
for it may not find it. The UI reads the product detail through a separate read-through
cache (ADR-0071) rather than through search, which hides most of this.

Rebuild cost puts a floor on how often mapping changes are viable. A mapping change is a
40-minute operation and is planned, never opportunistic.
