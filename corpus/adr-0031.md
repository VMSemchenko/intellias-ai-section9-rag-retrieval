# ADR-0031: Session cookie authentication (v3)

**Status:** Superseded by ADR-0042. Retained because four services still depend on it.

## Context

Every product surface needs to know who the caller is. In 2024 all of our clients were
first-party web applications served from the same parent domain, so a shared session
cookie was the cheapest correct answer.

## Decision

`auth-svc` issues an opaque session identifier on successful login and sets it as a
`Secure; HttpOnly; SameSite=Lax` cookie scoped to `.internal.example.com`. The session
record lives in Redis with a 12-hour sliding expiry.

Downstream services do not parse the cookie. They call `POST /v3/session/introspect`
with the raw cookie value and receive the subject, the tenant and the role set. The
introspection response is cacheable for 30 seconds.

## Consequences

The cookie is opaque, so revocation is immediate: delete the Redis key and the next
introspect call fails. That was the deciding factor over signed tokens.

The cost is a network hop. Every request that needs identity makes a synchronous call
to `auth-svc`. At the traffic we had in 2024 this was 3-4 ms and nobody noticed. By late
2025 `auth-svc` was handling more introspection traffic than every other endpoint
combined, and it became the single service whose availability capped the availability of
the whole platform.

The cookie also cannot leave the browser. Mobile clients and partner integrations have
no way to use this flow, which is what eventually forced ADR-0042.

## Migration

Do not build anything new against `/v3/session`. The deprecation timeline and the list
of remaining consumers are in the platform deprecation policy.
